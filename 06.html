OPERATION FULL SWING

TRISKICHAXI
LYCIA SYSTEM
2539

"Ai!"

The call is sharp, piercing. The hatchlings stop abruptly, turning to face the hulking figure which produced it with expressions ranging from chagrin to fear. Not the kind that comes with mortal peril, certainly, but the one that comes with the threat of consequence: broodguards supervising extra shifts cleaning the lavatories or stalking between desks as apologies are written out.

Triskichaxi puts on his best stern face. "How many times do I have to tell you?" He asks. "The algae pools are not for poking, or splashing, or leaning over so that your friends can get dangerously close to shoving you in, hmm?" He clicks his beak. "Not the first time. I recognize your faces. Pot washing not enough of a lesson?"

A series of vigorously shaking heads and squawked apologies. He narrows his eyes. "Am I going to see you here again?" he croaks. Assurances that no, no he won't. "Ai-yaa. Make me believe it." More enthusiasm now. It is hard to keep from laughing, but this is part of the lesson learned, his part to play in it. He lets out an almost theatrical sigh. "Then get a move on. I'm tired of looking at you." Claws click across the metal walkway as the hatchlings run past him and toward the exit. He waits until they've long since faded to let out a chuckle and shake his head, pushing the bitter ache that comes with it down, down down.

He was made for this. Well, not this, specifically: Babysitting is the job of the broodguards. But he was made to be imposing. Pitch-black feathers, nearly seven feet tall, beak hooked and sharp. Could take out an eye, his tenders used to say. It is not something he takes pride in, the fear. He admits to himself, sometimes, where nobody can hear, that he wishes that he could have been someone smaller. With a gentler purpose. A hatch-speaker, maybe, tasked with teaching the fresh fledges the basics of how the arkship’s denizens live. But he is what he has been built to be: a soldier. Not much threatens the brood now, but the present is only the present. There's no sense in assuming the future will be the same.

Especially not out here. The gears of war are always turning, but the skywatchers say they've been spinning even more quickly than usual. Triski straightens, enforcer uniform pulling somewhat uncomfortably around the shoulders. That's fine, he thinks. Inspiring fear should be a little uncomfortable.

An alarm sounds, a wailing keen that casts all thoughts of decorum to the side. He flicks a hand up toward his radio, but the skywatchers are already hissing through a forcibly opened communication channel.

“Arkship running,” they crackle. “Unknown pursuers, but the ark’s signature is familiar.”

The next words send a thrill of cold blood surging through Triski’s veins.

“Of Countless Stars and Sunrises.”

—

The bodies are so small.

He didn’t know bodies could be so small.

“Tris—”

“No.” His reply is harsh and final.

“Tel might be—”

“He isn’t. They let none escape. They didn’t—” He can’t finish the sentence. Even bred to be something cold, something weaponized, he doesn’t want to think about the ones they won’t be able to identify.

Telekii’s hatch-speaker stares up at him. His gaze doesn’t waver. Why, of the two of them, is the one tasked with all the bloodless parts of fledge-rearing the one who stands stable when confronted by the aftermath of the worst attack Of Victories Eternal has suffered in either of their memories?

The name sits bitter in the back of Triskichaxi’s mind. There is no victory here. They said he was ready, had earned his fledge. They had carefully copied his biometric data and select portions of memory into a child so small that he would have fit into one of the enforcer’s hands when they lifted him from the vats. A fledge that he was to raise, to train, with the help of the man who stands beside him. A man whose name he had never managed to commit to memory. He regrets that now, of course, but why bother now?

Now, when Triski is sure his fledge is one of the countless bodies left behind in the wake of an attack meant not to harm the arkship today but to disarm it in a generation’s time.

It hurts Triski today, though. And, out of the corner of his eye, he can see…

He remembers. Sharikkiki. Sha.

He can see the way that Sha’s hands shake as they curl into fists at his sides.

—

“We aren’t prepared.” The skywatcher’s head cranes backward to look up at Triski, who reaches above his minute form to retrieve the shotgun cached neatly above the empty rack that had held his hardsuit minutes before.

“Fuck your preparations,” Triski growls, beak snapping audibly around the consonants. “You remember what they did.”

“We have recovered from that attack,” the skywatcher replies. “Barely a setback. The brood was only a small one, and after we reached Lycia, we—” He is cut off abruptly by pressure and the cold sting of metal pressed through his feathers and against his flesh.

Triski holds the shotgun levelled at his throat. “Recovered?” he rattles. “You think we recovered?” The skywatcher is pushed backward, contorting around the hooks meant to hold a suit twice his size. “Tell me again just what you think we lost that day.”

To the smaller man’s credit, his voice only barely quavers as he responds. “You will call off the attack, Enforcer. Your life is not the only one you’re throwing away. Your entire wing, the others that have agreed to—”

“I asked you,” Triski hisses, “what you think we lost.”

The skywatcher’s face takes on an expression of irritation. “Nothing. We lost nothing. This system has proven bountiful. The lost generation is easily replaced.”

“Hmm.” Triski looks to the enforcers around him. His position offers him a solid vantage point: The winglead’s station stands elevated, giving them a very good view of those under their command. They all watch him with expressions that range from disinterest to hunger. No fear. No anger. No movement.

Good. He’ll still have an army when this is done.

“Replacable…” He rolls the word around in his mouth. “I suppose replacements await us all.” He turns his gaze back to the skywatcher, who has started to relax despite the barrel of the gun pressed into his throat.

“Yours won’t get here in time to stop me, though.”

The shot is loud in the enclosed space. The casing hits the floor with a metallic rattle, and Triski calmly reloads the weapon, replacing the sullied box of shells with a fresh one. He doesn’t bother to clean the blood off the muzzle of the gun, doesn’t bother to wipe it off the front of his suit. More will replace it soon enough. He turns his head.

“You have ten minutes,” he says. “I want all boots on shuttles before the vultures get here.”

He nudges the corpse out of his way with one of his feet then begins to make his final preparations. Revenge has been a long time coming. But he hasn’t been waiting idly. He’s ready, and the enemy is already running.

Even if he doesn’t have a place to come back to when this is done, he hopes Telekii is watching, somewhere. One last lesson.

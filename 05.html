Galactic History, Year 8: A NanoTrasen textbook
Chapter 12: Jackstadt-Derring and the Invisible War

As covered in the previous chapter, the 2520s-2540s were a period of intense corporate conflict between NanoTrasen and its opposition. While it spanned many sectors, this chapter will focus on events which occurred in the Lycia and Jackstadt-Derring systems, starting in the year 2539.

The Lycia system was a mineral-rich system bordering both NanoTrasen and Cybersun space. While NanoTrasen by law held claim to the sector, Cybersun felt that they had not been afforded enough of the nearby planetary systems and sought to encroach upon NanoTrasen claims. During the course of this conflict, Cybersun attacked a NanoTrasen research base working with experimental bluespace technology.† Doing so caused a catastrophic bluespace collapse, destroying the facility and opening a tear in spacetime. Combat moved into other sectors, as continuing to fight near such an unstable point in space was likely to result in unsustainable casualties on both sides. However, both companies saw potential value in exploring the rift left behind, sending research teams toward (and eventually into) what was soon designated a “Bluespace superhighway.”

NanoTrasen’s representative was Kedrick Jackstadt, the researcher responsible for the first major advances in Singularity-derived power generation. Aboard the Compass Rose, he and his team were the first to brave the unknown frontier. Once the passage had been deemed safe, Finnegan Derring led a Cybersun fleet spearheaded by the Centauri VI behind them. Both teams quickly realized that the four planets found in the system beyond the superhighway dwarfed the value of those in the Lycia system by several orders of magnitude, and for a time both groups operated peacefully under a research treaty. This, however, was disrupted when Derring’s team attempted to claim sole control of Jackstadt-Derring Habitable Planet I (or JDHP-1), the planet closest to the superhighway.††

Jackstadt-Derring quickly became a hotbed of conflict, but that conflict was not allowed to spill into or through the superhighway, as most ship-based weapons risked destabilizing it and trapping personnel in a sector the location of which was still an unknown as it related to inhabited space. Thus was the conflict named the Invisible War, as it went largely unnoticed by most of the population, with combat occurring in unmonitored territory. The Invisible War continued until the year 2546, when both NanoTrasen and Cybersun recognized that irreparable damage had already been done to the sector, and further fighting would push it past the point of no return. Thus, the Jackstadt-Derring Cybersun-Nanotrasen Non Compete Agreement, popularly referred to as Accord 2546, was signed.

ACCORD 2546

Under the agreement, parties agree to the following terms:

  1. No party will receive accord-granted control over more than half of the sector’s planets at the time of signing.
  2. No party should make an active attempt to establish greater financial, military, or political hold on a planet which another party holds that supercedes that of the controlling party.
  3. No party should engage in active combat with another party, threaten another party, or take any other aggressive action against another party unless the above terms are violated.

JDHP-1 and JDHP-4 were allotted to Cybersun, generously allowing them to settle the planet closest to the superhighway while still allowing NanoTrasen to levy taxes on shipments passing through the orbits of our planets, JDHP-2 and JDHP-3. In turn, Cybersun taxed vessels coming in and out of the superhighway. The agreement held until 2571, when Cybersun violated the terms of the agreement by establishing smaller, controlled Bluespace networks between JDHP-1 and JDHP-4, bypassing NanoTrasen space entirely.‡ As such, NanoTrasen was forced to levy their military power in the system to take control of the Cybersun planets, despite heavy resistance from the other corporation, which resulted in a staggering amount of avoidable casualties.‡‡
